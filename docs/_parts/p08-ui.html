Interface specifications

Wireframes with the states that matter. Two design laws govern everything here: operator screens are read at arm's length under time pressure, and guest screens must never lie about time.

6.1  Restaurant dashboard — the morning screen

The default view answers three questions in under five seconds: Is anything broken right now? How did yesterday go? What should I do today? Everything else is one click away. Metric cards carry a sparkline and a comparison, never a bare number — a number without context produces no decision.

 STATES · empty (pre-first-order: setup checklist replaces metrics)
        · degraded (a subsystem down: banner names it and says what still works)
        · offline (cloud unreachable: "Running locally — 12 orders queued", never a spinner)
        · multi-location (metric cards aggregate; a location selector switches scope)

 DESIGN NOTES
  · NEEDS YOU is the only red on the screen. If everything is fine, the block collapses
    to a single green line. Alert fatigue is the failure mode; three items is the cap.
  · Sparklines are 7-day, same-weekday-aligned. Week-over-week, never day-over-day —
    restaurant volume is weekday-periodic and DoD comparisons are noise.
  · AI suggestions live in a fixed rail, never as an interstitial or a modal. They are
    an offer, not an interruption.
  · Touch targets ≥ 44px throughout: this is used on a tablet, on a prep counter,
    by someone holding a phone in the other hand.

6.2  Kitchen display

The most latency- and legibility-critical screen in the system. Read from 5–8 feet, in heat and steam, by someone who cannot touch it with clean hands. Everything is oversized, high-contrast, and colored by time remaining against the promise — not by status.

 COLOR = TIME AGAINST PROMISE, nothing else
   normal (>2 min headroom) · warning (<2 min) · late (past promise)
   Courier-waiting state is shown on the ticket itself, because a waiting driver
   is the single most expensive状 state in the system.

 INTERACTIONS · bump = large tap or a physical bump bar · recall restores with
   full history · long-press an item to 86 it (propagates to marketplace in <20s)
   · no confirmation dialogs anywhere: every action is undoable instead
 CONSTRAINTS · must render from the LAN with zero internet · never sleeps ·
   survives a 14-hour session without a reload · audible alert on new + on late

6.4  Checkout

Every fee is visible before the guest commits. The competitive claim of this product is price honesty; a hidden service fee revealed on the last screen destroys it permanently, and no growth metric is worth that.

 THE RULES THAT MAKE THIS DIFFERENT
  · Every timestamp is real. The bar advances on events, never on a timer.
  · ETA is a range, and it widens rather than sliding silently. If it slips,
    the guest is told once, with a reason ("kitchen is backed up"), not four times.
  · Signal loss shows "Last updated 2 min ago" over a static marker. A moving dot
    that isn't real is a lie the guest will eventually catch.
  · Courier position is coarsened near the delivery address; the guest's exact
    location is never exposed to the courier beyond the drop instruction.
  · Batched deliveries say so: "One stop before yours." Hiding it is how trust dies.

 PRIVACY BOUNDARIES ENFORCED IN THIS UI
  · Cross-brand behavior is NEVER shown here. An operator sees their own relationship
    only. Network-level insight appears only as anonymized cohort stats, min n = 25.
  · Consent state is displayed with its timestamp on every profile; a guest without
    consent cannot be selected into a marketing audience — the checkbox is disabled,
    with the reason on hover, rather than silently dropped at send time.
  · "Delete data" runs a real DSAR cascade across Postgres, ClickHouse, and the
    feature store, and returns a receipt.

 HOW THIS DIFFERS FROM A GENERIC AI SITE BUILDER
  · It edits a structured page model, not raw HTML. "Add a catering section" inserts a
    typed section with real fields, so the site stays valid, fast, and re-themeable.
  · The menu is live-bound to the menu of record. Publishing a price in the POS updates
    the website in under 20 seconds. There is no second copy of the menu to go stale.
  · SEO is a checklist with fixes attached, not a score. Every warning has a one-click action.
  · Every generated string passes the claims filter before it can be published.
  · Ordering is native to the page — no redirect to a third-party ordering domain, which is
    what costs conversion and SEO authority on every competing product.
