Pitch deck outline

Fourteen slides for a seed raise. Each entry states the slide's single claim and the evidence that has to sit under it — a deck where any slide carries two claims is a deck that will get interrupted.

01
Title
Claim
Mobile Dinners — the operating system independent restaurants run on, and the marketplace their customers order from.
On screen
Logo, one line, city, date. Nothing else. The line has to survive being repeated by a partner to their partners without the deck present.
02
Problem
Claim
Independent restaurants rent their own customers back from delivery apps at 20–30%, and run six disconnected systems to do it.
Evidence
One real P&L from a design partner with the aggregator fee line highlighted. A screenshot of the six logins one owner actually uses. Restaurant net margin of 3–5% against a 28% commission — the comparison makes itself.
Avoid
Market-size slides disguised as problem slides. Show one owner's actual bank statement, redacted.
03
Solution
Claim
One system replaces all six, and adds a marketplace that charges zero commission on food.
On screen
The three-plane diagram (Fig. 00.1), simplified to three boxes. Operations → Growth → Demand, with the sentence: each plane produces the data the next one needs.
04
Product — live demo
Claim
This is built and running in real restaurants today.
Demo script
90 seconds, in this order: (1) place an order in the consumer app; (2) the ticket appears on a real KDS on the table; (3) unplug the network cable and place another order — it still works; (4) plug back in and watch it reconcile. The unplug moment is the whole demo. Rehearse it forty times.
05
Why now
Claim
Three things became true at once: aggregator commissions hit the ceiling of what restaurants tolerate, POS APIs opened enough to allow coexistence, and AI collapsed the cost of the onboarding work that made this category impossible to scale.
Evidence
Fee-cap legislation in multiple cities. Menu ingestion that took 6 hours of human labor per restaurant in 2019 now takes 3 minutes — and onboarding cost was the reason vertical SaaS never reached the long tail of independents.
06
Market
Claim
~750k US restaurants; ~500k independent. At a blended $742/location/month, a 5% share is roughly $2.2B ARR.
Discipline
Show SAM as independents in metros above 300k population with existing delivery penetration — roughly 180k locations. A credible bottom-up SAM beats a $500B TAM slide with every serious investor, and gets you eaten alive by the unserious ones. Optimize for the former.
07
Competition
Claim
Four categories exist; none of them spans operations, growth, and demand.
On screen
The comparison table from §0, reduced to five rows. Axes: owns the kitchen × owns the customer. POS vendors own the kitchen only. Growth tools own the customer only. Aggregators own demand and rent it back. Mobile Dinners is the only entry in the upper-right quadrant.
Honesty
Name Toast, Square, Owner.com, Slice, ChowNow, and DoorDash explicitly. An investor who knows the space will ask; being first to name them buys credibility.
08
Differentiation — the closed loop
Claim
Mobile Dinners is the only party that sees cross-restaurant consumer behavior and per-item margin inside the kitchen. That intersection produces decisions nobody else can make.
Proof
A single worked example: a specific guest, a specific item, a specific hour, and the margin math behind the recommendation — then the measured lift against the holdout.
09
Traction
Claim
Restaurants are paying, staying, and shifting real order volume to channels they own.
Metrics that matter
Paying locations; logo and dollar retention by monthly cohort; direct-order share (the leading indicator of the entire thesis); incremental revenue per location, holdout-verified; consumer MAU and repeat rate; zone density.
Metrics to omit
GMV, signups, app installs, "restaurants in pipeline." Vanity metrics on a traction slide signal that the real ones are weak.
10
Business model
Claim
Five revenue lines; SaaS and payments carry the margin, delivery is run to roughly breakeven as an acquisition channel rather than a profit center.
On screen
The per-order contribution table from §0 alongside the revenue-line table. State the delivery economics honestly — an investor who has funded a delivery company will test this exact number, and a candid answer is far stronger than an optimistic one.
11
Go-to-market
Claim
Win one neighborhood at a time. Restaurants first, consumers acquired inside those restaurants at single-digit CAC.
On screen
The zone density ladder, with metro one's actual current stage marked. Land-and-expand from Starter to Growth. CAC and payback by channel.
12
Team
Claim
This team has shipped payments-grade infrastructure and has operated or sold into restaurants.
Requirement
If nobody on the founding team has worked a service window, hire or advise that gap and say so on the slide. Restaurant tech punishes outsiders, and investors in this category know it.
13
Ask & use of funds
Claim
$8–10M seed for 18 months to reach 600 locations across three metros with positive delivery contribution in two zones.
Allocation
~55% engineering and product, ~25% go-to-market, ~10% hardware working capital, ~10% general and administrative. Name the three milestones the round buys, and the Series A metrics they produce.
14
Vision
Claim
Every independent restaurant runs on infrastructure it controls, and every neighborhood has a marketplace that keeps the money in it.
Landing line
State the ten-year shape in one sentence — the operating system for the independent restaurant economy — then stop talking. Do not add an appendix slide to the vision slide.

Severity is expected impact on company survival, not probability.

The one-sentence version

Mobile Dinners is a bet that the restaurant operating system is the wedge and the marketplace is the moat. Build the wedge until 150 restaurants pay for it on its own merits, then and only then spend money teaching consumers a new app.
